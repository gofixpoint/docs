---
title: "Embed Amika in your product"
description: "Give your own users machines for coding agents, with Amika as the infrastructure."
---

Amika can be the machine layer inside your product. Your users get their own
[rigs](/guides/what-is-a-rig): isolated machines where their coding agents,
dev servers, and tools run. You build the product experience. Amika runs the
machines, keeps each user's work apart, and gives you one API to control it.

## Why embed Amika

Running agent machines for many users is hard to do well. Each user needs a
machine that boots fast, holds their code, and stays isolated from every
other user. You also need SSH, preview URLs, and a way to cut off access when
a user leaves.

Amika gives you all of that as a platform:

- **Machines per user.** Each of your users gets rigs that only they can use.
  Other users can't see them, and your own team can't run code on them.
- **Any agent, any model.** Run Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Pi, or your own
  harness on the rig.
- **Full machine control.** Each rig is a real VM with root, SSH, and
  services. It is not a sandboxed function call.
- **Their compute or yours.** Rigs run on Amika Cloud, or on machines your
  users connect themselves with [bring your own compute](/guides/byoc).
- **One API.** Your backend uses the same [HTTP API](/reference/http-api-overview)
  and [TypeScript SDK](/sdks/overview) that Amika's own CLI uses.

## Who embeds Amika

- **Coding agent products.** Give each customer a persistent machine for your
  agent, instead of building your own VM fleet.
- **Developer platforms and IDEs.** Offer cloud dev environments that open in
  seconds, with previews and SSH.
- **Education and assessment.** Give each student or candidate a clean,
  isolated machine, and remove access when the course or interview ends.
- **Internal platforms.** Give each team or contractor their own machines, kept
  apart from the rest of your organization.

## How it fits together

Your users never need an Amika account. Your organization holds the Amika
account, and your users are **end-users** inside it:

1. Your backend authenticates your user the way it does today.
2. Your backend calls Amika with an API key, either acting as that user or
   minting a short-lived token for your client app.
3. Amika creates and runs the user's rigs, and enforces that only that user
   can use them.

End-users are off by default. To turn them on for your organization, contact
Amika.

[End-user access](/platform/end-user-access) explains the access model, the
keys and tokens, and what each caller can do.
